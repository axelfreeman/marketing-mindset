How do you price your first client without data?

Short answer: Price the outcome, not the hours: one number you can defend plus one premium option. Your first client's price is a positioning decision, not a calculation - so make it deliberately.

With no case studies there is no formula. What you do have is the value of the thing you are replacing or unlocking - use that.

Price the outcome, then anchor it

  • What is the result worth? If it produces 5 qualified conversations a month and your client closes 1 in 5 at 4,000, one conversation is roughly 800. Price against that, not against your hours.
  • Anchor with a real alternative: an in-house hire, an agency retainer, or doing nothing. Your price sits in relation to one of those three, so name it in the conversation.
  • Say the number once, then stop talking. The silence after the price is the negotiation.

Only two options

Three tiers turn a decision into a research project. Two options force a choice:

OptionWhat it is for
CoreThe outcome you can personally promise in the timeframe. This is your price.
Core + done-with-youSame outcome plus your time in their stack. 1.5-2x. It exists to make the core option look obvious.

Rules that protect the first engagement

  • Never discount without removing scope. A discount with the same scope teaches the client that your price was fiction.
  • Charge for the first month up front. It filters buyers from browsers better than any qualification call.
  • Write the kill rule into the contract: what you deliver, in what volume, by when, and what happens if it does not land. Nobody argues about the invoice when the threshold was agreed.

Your first price is not a valuation of your work. It is the position you will have to hold for the next twenty clients - so choose the position, not the compromise.
